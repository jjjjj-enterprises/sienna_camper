A

Check before you drill

2 checks · 2 methods

The tray's right side, the kitchen side, hangs from the kitchen-side slide: apron → moving rail → fixed rail → kitchen riser → middle strip. The left side is the mirror image on the driver strip.

Check The slides came without screws

VADANIA sells the VD2576 without hardware and calls for M6 screws. A ¼-20 machine screw is the inch equivalent and fits the same holes. Measure a hole in your rails and try a ¼-20 in it before buying.

Into the steel: ¼-20 flat-head machine screws with lock nuts. Into the wood aprons: #12 × ⅝" flat-head wood screws.

Check Keep the apron screws ⅝" or shorter

The aprons are ¾" thick and the fridge sits about 1/16" inside them. A ¾" screw would come through into the fridge's side.

Method The rails hang 1/16" above the board

Resting on the board, the moving rail would scrape the strip every time the tray slides. Set the fixed rail's bottom edge 1/16" above the flange's underside (the board's top) with a shim, a scrap of cardboard or two playing cards, while you mark and drill.

board top 0.85" + 1/16" = rail bottom 15/16" · rail 3" · tray hangs ⅜" · apron overlap ≈ 1⅞" (needs 1½")

Method Drill the steel slow and wet

Center-punch every mark, clamp the angle down, drill a ⅛" pilot, then open it to 9/32". Low speed, firm pressure, a drop of cutting fluid in each hole. Deburr both sides with a bigger bit turned by hand.

F

Build order

bench first, van last

Check the screws fit

Pull the slides apart (most have a release lever on the moving member). Try a ¼-20 screw in a fixed-rail hole and a moving-rail hole. If it won't pass, buy M6 instead.

Drill the flange holes

Both risers, four each at 2", 8½", 15½" and 22" from the tailgate end: 1" from the back of the upright on the driver riser, ⅝" on the kitchen riser (Fig 4). Punch, ⅛" pilot, 9/32", deburr.

Fit the fixed rails to the risers

Riser flange-down on a flat bench. Stand the fixed rail against the upright's inner face (the side away from the flange) on a 1/16" shim, ends flush with the riser's ends. Clamp, mark through the rail's holes that land on the upright, drill 9/32", and bolt with the flat heads inside the rail and lock nuts outside. Slide the moving rail back on and check it runs past every screw head.

Drill and seat the T-nuts

Lay each riser on its strip (Fig 5), mark through the flange holes, and drill 5/16" straight through. Turn the board over, counterbore each hole ¾" just deep enough for the T-nut's flange, and tap the T-nuts in flush. Test each with a bolt.

Moving rails on the aprons

Tray on its side. Each moving rail's bottom edge 9/16" above the apron's bottom, its tailgate end 2½" from the apron's tailgate end (Fig 6). Mark through its holes, ⅛" pilots no deeper than ⅝", and #12 × ⅝" flat heads.

In the van: risers down, tray in

Board on its mat and tongues pinned. Bolt both risers down. Engage the tray's moving rails into the fixed rails and push it home. Pull it out to full extension and back: it should lock both ways, clear the rear leg, and pass under Panel C's rear rail.

Geometry from params.scad (fridge_rail_t, fridge_tray_gap, aboard_*) and the Riser Angles and Fridge Tray sheets: driver apron's outer face 2½" from Panel C's driver edge, tray 19⅜" overall, board top 0.85" with a 0.1" mat. Where the model and this sheet differ: the model stands each rail on its riser's flange, which puts the rail bottom at 1.1"; with the flanges pointing outboard the rail stands beside the upright instead, 1/16" above the board, which gives the apron screws about ⅜" more band.
