A

Check before you drill

2 checks · 2 methods

The tray's right side, the kitchen side, hangs from the kitchen-side slide: apron → moving rail → fixed rail → kitchen riser → middle strip. The left side is the mirror image on the driver strip.

Check The slides don't come apart, and may bring their own bolts

VADANIA's directions mount each slide in one piece: press the lock button and pull the rail out to expose the screw holes, screw the outer (fixed) rail to the cabinet, then set the drawer in place and screw the inner (moving) rail to it, sliding the rail along to reach each hole. They call for "bolts, nuts, or other suitable fasteners from the accessory pack".

The slides came with their bolts and nuts: use those for the fixed rails into the steel risers. Into the wood aprons: #12 × ⅝" flat-head wood screws, unless the pack has short wood screws of its own.

Check Keep the apron screws ⅝" or shorter

The aprons are ¾" thick and the fridge sits about 1/16" inside them. A ¾" screw would come through into the fridge's side.

Method The rails hang 1/16" above the board

Resting on the board, the moving rail would scrape the strip every time the tray slides. Set the fixed rail's bottom edge 1/16" above the flange's underside (the board's top) with a shim while you mark and drill: a piece of cereal-box cardboard or two playing cards is about 1/16".

board top 0.85" + 1/16" = rail bottom 15/16" · rail 3" · tray hangs ⅜" · apron overlap ≈ 1⅞" (needs 1½")

Method Drill the steel slow and wet

Center-punch every mark and clamp the angle down. Pilot with the ⅛" Thunderbolt, then open the hole with step bit #1 until the 9/32" step (the sixth from the tip) finishes cutting. The 3/16" steel is taller than one step, so flip the angle and touch the hole from the other side to straighten it, then kiss both faces with the next step to deburr. Low speed, firm pressure, a drop of Tap Magic in each hole.

G

Build order

bench first, van last

Check the hardware and the lock end

Open the slides' accessory pack: bolts and nuts for the steel. Press the lock button and pull one slide out to full length to see the holes. The lock button goes at the tailgate end on both slides, so you can reach it with the tray out. Try a bolt in a hole of each rail. Measure the bolt's threads, not its head: about ¼" means M6, which the 9/32" holes suit.

Drill the flange holes

Both risers, four each at 2", 8½", 15½" and 22" from the tailgate end: 1" from the back of the upright on the driver riser, ⅝" on the kitchen riser (Fig 4). Punch, ⅛" pilot, 9/32", deburr.

Fixed rails onto the risers

Riser flange-down on a flat bench. Stand the whole slide against the back of the L: the upright's flat face that has no flange on that side, so the flange points away from the slide. Put a 1/16" shim under the slide (a piece of cereal-box cardboard or two playing cards) so its bottom edge sits 1/16" above the bottom of the L (the bench). Ends flush with the riser's ends, lock button at the tailgate end. Clamp it. Slide the moving rail along to expose each fixed-rail hole that lands on the upright, mark, drill 9/32", and bolt: heads inside the rail, nuts outside. Run the slide its full travel to check it clears every head.

Drill and seat the T-nuts

Lay each riser on its strip (Fig 5), mark through the flange holes, and drill 5/16" straight through. Turn the board over, counterbore each hole ¾" just deep enough for the T-nut's flange, and tap the T-nuts in flush. Test each with a bolt.

On the garage floor: board on its mat, risers loose

Flat floor, the rubber mat down, the board on it top face up: the mat sets the board at its van height. Both risers on their strips, flanges pointing outward, slides facing each other across the gap, lock buttons at the strip tips. Start the eight ¼-20 × 1" bolts finger-tight so the risers can still shift.

Set the tray between the slides

Two ⅜" spacers on the floor in the gap (scraps that stack to ⅜"), the tray on them. Slide it fore-aft until the slides' tailgate ends sit 2½" in from the aprons' tailgate ends; the tray's tailgate end then sits about 2" past the strip tips. Push the risers in until each slide lies flat on its apron, and check its bottom edge is about 9/16" above the apron's bottom (Fig 6).

First screws, tray closed

Tape a depth stop on the ⅛" bit ⅝" from its tip. At a moving-rail hole you can reach near the front of each slide, pilot and drive a #12 × ⅝" flat head. The tray now hangs on both slides.

Work along the rest of the holes

Pull the spacers. Draw the tray out a few inches at a time; more moving-rail holes show through the openings in the other rail sections. Pilot and screw each one that lands on the apron.

Square it up, then tighten

Run the tray out to the lock and back to the lock several times so the loose risers settle where the tray wants them. Close it and tighten the eight riser bolts. Run it again: smooth, no binding, locked at both ends.

Unbolt it for the van

Undo the eight riser bolts: the tray, slides and risers lift off as one unit. In the van it goes mat, board, tongues, then the unit back on its T-nuts and bolted down. Test full travel there: clear of Panel C's rear leg and under its rear rail with the fridge on, then load and strap the fridge. Re-check the riser bolts after the first drive.

Geometry from params.scad (fridge_rail_t, fridge_tray_gap, aboard_*) and the Riser Angles and Fridge Tray sheets: driver apron's outer face 2½" from Panel C's driver edge, tray 19⅜" overall, board top 0.85" with a 0.1" mat. Where the model and this sheet differ: the model stands each rail on its riser's flange, which puts the rail bottom at 1.1"; with the flanges pointing outboard the rail stands beside the upright instead, 1/16" above the board, which gives the apron screws about ⅜" more band.
