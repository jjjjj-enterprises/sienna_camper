A

Check before you cut

1 check · 1 method · 2 carry-overs

The tray is three pieces and a dozen screws. What matters is its width, where the aprons go, and where it ends up in Panel C.

Check Size the panel to your fridge, not the listing

The plan sizes the panel at 17.72", the Rocky 40's listed width. The aprons' top edges are the fridge's side stops, so at exactly the fridge's width there is no room to set it in.

Measure the fridge across its base and add ⅛". At the listed width that's 17⅞". The length stays 28¾", the fridge's length over its end handles.

Method The aprons go on the panel's edges, not under it

The fridge has almost no headroom. On the tray it tops out at 16.67", and Panel C's rear rail is 17-1/16" off the floor at deck 18½". That leaves 0.39", and the plan wants at least ¼" for bounce. Anything under the panel, aprons or slides, raises the fridge into the rail. Undermount slides alone would add about 1.2".

So the aprons are glued and screwed to the panel's long edges, with their bottoms flush with its underside (Fig 3). The panel hangs ⅜" off the floor, and each slide's moving rail screws to the outside face of an apron.

Carry-over The slides have to sit clear of the rear leg

The plan puts the driver-side apron against Panel C's driver rear leg, with the slide in front of the leg and its fixed rail set back 2½" from the tailgate. Closed, that clears. Pulled out, the moving rail runs out the tailgate through the leg's footprint, and the leg stops it.

Move the whole tray 1" inboard: the driver apron's outer face 2½" from Panel C's driver edge, against the edge of the anchor board's driver strip, so the slide sits on that strip just inboard of the leg (Figs 1 and 2). That leaves about 1-3/16" between the kitchen-side slide and the kitchen unit at floor level. None of this changes what you cut.

Carry-over Don't fit the slides yet

The fixed rails bolt to steel riser angles on the anchor board, the ply comb on the van floor. The comb is already cut, and the gap between its driver strip and its middle strip is 16.85", narrower than the fridge. The tray can't be raised over the strips instead, because Panels A and B can't come up to meet a higher Panel C. So on 13 Oct the comb was cut: the middle strip trimmed to 2⅛" wide, starting 21⅞" from the driver edge, and the crossbar cut down to about 3¼" so it ends ⅛" short of the front wall. The tray hangs between the strips as planned (Fig 2). The board's hardware isn't bought yet.

Build the tray now and stop before the slides. How high the moving rail sits on the apron depends on how thick the board and risers come out, and the screws need at least 1½" of overlap with the apron.

G

Build order

about an hour, plus glue time
Done

Measure the fridge

Across its base, along its length over the handles, and where each end handle sits. The panel is the base width plus ⅛".

Done

Cut the panel

17⅞ × 28¾" (or your width) from the ½" leftover. Check it's square: the diagonals should match, about 33⅞" at 17⅞".

Done

Rip and cut the two aprons

Rip a 1×4 to 2½", then crosscut two pieces at 28¾", the same length as the panel. Sight down each one and put any bow facing up, so the bow sits away from the floor.

Done

Dry-fit on a flat floor

Panel flat on the floor, an apron against each long edge, ends flush. The floor makes the bottoms flush. Set the fridge in: it should drop between the aprons with a little play each side.

Done

Glue and screw the aprons

Glue each edge, clamp the apron on, and check the bottoms are still flush. Pilot 3/32" into the ply edge, ¼" up from the bottom, at 1½" from each end and five more evenly between (about 5⅛" apart). Countersink and drive 6 × 1¼" per side, heads flush (Fig 3).

Clean up

Wipe off the squeeze-out on the inside corners before it sets, so the fridge sits flat on the panel. When the glue has cured, ease the edges with sandpaper. A coat of finish is optional.

Mark and fit the D-rings

Set the fridge on, compressor end toward the tailgate end of the tray. Mark the inboard apron's top edge under each end handle, pilot, and turn in the two screw eyes.

Stop here

Slides wait for the anchor board

The moving rails screw to the aprons' outer faces, at a height set by the anchor board and its risers, with at least 1½" of each rail on the apron. The anchor board gets its own sheet first.

This sheet adds ⅛" to the width and moves the tray 1-1/16" inboard so the driver slide clears the leg at full extension and the tray hangs between the cut comb's strips. The model's open issue about the apron's ¾" thickness is settled here the way the plan's section V2 draws it: rail outside the apron, apron against the panel.
