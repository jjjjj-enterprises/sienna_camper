A

What changed, and why

3 changes · 1 consequence

Dropped The exhaust fan

The plan put it "on the kitchen-facing wall," but there is no wall there: between the fridge's passenger slide rail and the kitchen unit is a 1⅝" gap, and a fan aimed across it blows into the kitchen's side. The fridge slides out the tailgate end, so a fan can't sit there either. One intake fan pushes the air instead, and the second fan is a spare. If the bay runs hot, it can go push-pull on the same hole later, on the Panel B face (about 1.1" deep with a guard). No cutting either way.

Moved The control cluster, passenger → driver column

The passenger site overlapped the passenger front leg, which stands 1-7/16" proud of the wall. The driver column (between the driver leg and where Panel B's totes start, x 1½–6") is clear, and it sits right above the grommet the wires use, close to the fan and the fridge.

Fixed W1209 mode C, not H

H is heating mode: the relay closes when it's cold. The fans want C (cooling): on when the bay warms up.

Consequence Panels B and C no longer lift straight past each other

Panel C's front legs hold Panel B 1-7/16" off the wall. The cluster stands up to 2¾" out, into the plane of Panel B's end rails. Lifted straight up, B's bottom end rail would hit it, and C lifted straight up would hit B's top end rail. To take them out: lift Panel A first, slide Panel B 3" forward, then lift it. Everyday use doesn't change.

F

Build order

bench now · van later

Mount the fan

On the plenum side of the existing hole, arrow on the frame pointing toward the fridge, finger guard on the Panel B side. Four M4 × 50mm screws and nylon lock nuts (40mm is too short: the stack is about 44mm). Run its lead along the plenum face to the grommet and through. The fan comes with a small 2-pin plug (red +, black −). Cut it off about 2" from the plug and keep the plug end for a spare-fan swap; the fan's leads are thin (~26AWG), so splice each to 18AWG with a solder joint and heat shrink (or fold the stripped end double before crimping). Red → W1209 K1, black → the NEG bus.

Mount the cluster

Fuse block on end at z 5", W1209 above it, switch plate on its 2x2 block at the top (Switch Plate sheet): all in the driver column, x 1½–6". #8 × ⅞" screws for the fuse block and W1209, #8 × 2¼" for the switch plate, all stopping inside the ½" wall so nothing comes through on the plenum side. Check the stack stays inside 2¾" deep.

Pre-wire on the bench

P6, P9, P10 and P12 (Fig 4). 18AWG red and black in the bay, ring terminals on the block's studs, ¼" female spades on the switch tabs. Crimp, tug-test, shrink. Jumper the W1209's +12V to K0. Leave the DC line's end and the fridge feed loose for the van.

Bench test and set the W1209

Feed 12V to the block's +IN and −IN (the DELTA's car outlet through the SAE adapter). SW2 on. Set mode C, the setpoint about 30°C (86°F), and the hysteresis about 5°C, so it switches on near 35°C (95°F). Warm the probe in your hand and watch the relay LED and the fan; adjust until it does that. Fan off, SW1 on: the fridge feed should be live.

In the van: power in

Bring the fridge DC line from the B/C SAE (P3) to the block's +IN and −IN (P5): #10 ring terminals, M5 nuts.

In the van: fridge feed and probe

With the slides installed, run the fridge feed and the probe lead through the grommet and back along the driver fixed rail. Put the socket near the fridge's power port at the louvered end, with the slack loop clipped to the fixed rail (P7–P8). Zip-tie the probe tip at the rail's tailgate end, about 1" from the louvered end, in the moving air, not touching metal.

Run test

Fridge on, everything closed up as for driving. After 30 minutes of the compressor running, the W1209 should read the bay's warm air and the fan should cycle on and off. If it runs nonstop and the reading keeps climbing, that's the cue for the push-pull second fan.
