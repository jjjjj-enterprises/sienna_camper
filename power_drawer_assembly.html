A

Check before you cut

2 fixed · 1 still open

Five stale dimensions were corrected in sienna_camper_build_plan.md and cut_list.html in September 2026, and the PDF was rebuilt — the first two entries below record why, and the plan now matches this sheet. The third is still open and needs a tape measure on the frame.

Fixed The 14½" wall height predated the deck recess

The cut list and Component 2's parts list both called the four walls 14½" tall. Panel A's bay is not that tall any more. When the deck recess dropped Panels A and B's legs by ¾" (deck_drop = panel_thickness), the bay went with them:

bay = leg_height_ab 16.25 − top rail 1.5 − bottom rail 1.0 = 13.75"

And 14½" is exactly what that same expression gave before the recess: 17 − 1.5 − 1.0 = 14.5. The wall height simply never followed the legs down.

Cut the walls 13¾", not 14½". A 14½" box is ¾" too tall to go under the deck at all. — now corrected in the plan, the cut list and the PDF.

Knock-on worth checking: Section 3 argues the walls must be ¾" ply because "nothing left on the ½" sheet has a 14.5" dimension." At 13¾" that argument needs re-running — if the ½" sheet's offcuts do hold them, the drawer sheds roughly 6 lb of the ~12 lb the ¾" walls cost, on a slide pair whose margin the plan already calls thin.

Settled 22" + 20" walls can't both run full length

The cut list gives two side walls at 22" and two front/back walls at 20" — the box's full depth and its full width. On a 20 × 22 outer box only one pair can run the whole way; the other has to be short by two wall thicknesses.

22 − 2(¾) = 20½ clear depth · 20 − 2(¾) = 18½ clear width ✓ matches the plan's own clear figures

The plan never said which pair gives way. Settled (owner, Sept 2026): the 22" side walls run the full depth, and the two 20" front/back walls are cut back. That is also the right way round structurally — the slides screw to the side walls' outer faces, and a slide wants one continuous, full-length wall under it, not a wall interrupted at both ends by end grain.

20 − 1½ = 18½" · one cut per piece, ×2 · 1½ = two wall thicknesses (2 × ¾), NOT 1½ off each end

Final: sides 2 @ 22 × 13¾, front/back 2 @ 18½ × 13¾. Cut the side walls first and trim the front/back pair to the box they actually make — ¾" ply runs over and under its nominal thickness, and 1/32" at each corner is 1/16" out of square.

Open As built, the frame reads 16" floor to rail bottom

Measured on the assembled frame, feet included — the model expects 16¼". Because the legs are lapped flush with the rail top and the bottom rail hangs off the leg's wood bottom, that ¼" splits into two questions that have different answers:

floor→rail bottom = foot exposure f + leg wood L − 1.5 = 16.0" → f + L = 17.5"

The deck plane depends on f — and f is adjustable. Rail top is sitting at 17½" instead of 17¾", so the bed platform lands at 18¼", a ¼" below Panel C's fixed 18½" deck: a step at the B/C seam, directly under the mattress. Turn all four feet out ¼" and it is gone. This is exactly what the leveling feet are for, and it costs nothing.

The drawer bay does not depend on f at all. The bottom rail rises and falls with the leg, so the bay is set by the leg's wood length alone:

bay = L − 1.5 (top rail) − 1.5 (bottom rail) = L − 3

So it comes down to what the legs were actually cut to:

• L = 16¾" as specified → bay 13¾", this sheet stands exactly as drawn, and the feet are simply threaded in to ¾" instead of 1".
• L = 16½" (cut ¼" short) → bay 13½": walls 13½", clear height 13", dead air over the stack 1.84" — and the lift-out tray comes down to 1½" or less.

One measurement settles it: the clear opening in the bay — top of the bottom rail up to the underside of the top rail. It reads either 13¾" or 13½". Everything on this sheet follows from that number, and it is unaffected by however you end up setting the feet.

G

Assembly order

Component 2 step 3 → Component 8 step 1
Do this first

Cut the anchor board comb

Two of the four walls are its gaps, not its offcuts — gap A gives a side wall, gap B a front/back wall. Jigsaw or router the comb out of the 46 × 33 blank before anything here gets cut to size.

Cut the five pieces

Walls to the bay height — 13¾", or 13½" if Flag 3's bay measurement says so. Side walls 22", front/back trimmed 1½" to 18½" (Flag 2). Bottom 20 × 22 out of the ½" sheet. Label each piece the moment it comes off the saw — two of them arrive rotated out of the comb and the grain direction is not recoverable by eye later.

Rabbet the four walls for the bottom

⅜" deep × ½" wide along the bottom inside edge of all four walls, so the ½" bottom lands flush with the box's underside. Cut these before the biscuit slots — it is far easier on a loose board.

Slot the corners — 2 × R3 each

Eight slots in total, centred on the ¾" thickness, 2" in from each end of the joint. R3, not R1: R1 is thin-stock sizing and disappears in ¾" ply. Set the DBJ50's fence to 90° and check the depth on an offcut first.

Dry-fit the whole box

All four walls plus the bottom, no glue. Measure both diagonals — they should match. This is the last cheap moment to find out that the wall height or the corner convention is off.

Glue up

Glue every biscuit slot and the mating faces, and the bottom's rabbet all the way round. Clamp, re-check the diagonals before the glue grabs, and leave it. The bottom is structural here — it is what stops a 48 lb load racking the box on the slide.

Hang it on the slides

20" full-extension pair: one slide to the frame's outer rail, the other to Panel A's centre divider, both on the side walls' outer faces. Run it out fully and back a few times loaded with a sandbag before you trust it with the batteries.

Add the catch

Friction catch or a small turn latch. Not optional — 48 lb rolling itself open on a corner is the failure that takes the slide with it.

Fit the drawer's fixtures

Four 1×1 cleats glued and screwed to the floor, bracketing the 15.95 × 15.7" footprint. Two screw-eye D-rings at the side walls on the strap's line. The 1" grommet goes in a fore-aft wall — the side walls' outer faces belong to the slides — with enough cable slack for full extension.

Load and strap

DELTA 3 Plus outboard at the pull wall, Smart Extra Battery inboard, both down over the cleats. Cam strap across the width, through both D-rings, cinched. Shallow tray on top last, if you build one.

Connect it

Fridge DC line to the Plus's 12V car-power outlet via its fused car-plug→SAE adapter (connection point P1), and the AC charging cord to the AC input. From here the drawer runs the fridge and fans continuously and recharges off the front console while you drive.
