The drawing is your comb as measured: the crossbar cut down to 4" deep, each 24" riser ⅝" in from the strip's hatch end, and the comb 30⅞" overall. Measure the back line from the riser's end, not the strip's: ½" forward of the riser is 1⅛" from the strip's end. Measured from the strip's end instead, both lines come out ⅝" toward the hatch and the front lands 1⅝" short of the riser. The risers are still the reference for the marks. The riser is 24" long. The strip keeps ½" + 22½" + 1" of it: ½" of riser hangs past the strip's back end and 1" past its front end.

If the front line doesn't land 1" short of the riser's front end, stop: something's off.

As built The two risers sit ⅛" apart on the comb

The driver riser starts ½" in from its strip's hatch end; the middle riser starts ⅝" in (owner, 25 Oct). Mark each strip off its own riser, so the lines are the same from the riser and ⅛" different from the plywood:

from the riser's hatch end: back ½" · front 23" (both strips)
from the plywood's hatch end: driver 1" · 23½" · middle 1⅛" · 23⅝"

Each strip still comes out 22½" with its riser ½" past the back and 1" past the front, so once the strips are separate the two risers line up and the ⅛" disappears. The passenger strip has no riser: mark it 22½" long anywhere in its length. It lines up with the others by its back end when the strips are laid out for the grooves.

Check Both cut lines in plain strip

The front line has to fall behind the crossbar, in the strip itself. On your comb it lands 3¼" behind the crossbar's inner edge (7¼" from its outer edge), so there's room. The back line takes 1⅛" off the hatch end: the ⅝" of strip behind the riser, plus the ½" the riser now overhangs.

Check The T-nuts stay out of the grooves

From the new back line the riser's T-nuts should sit at about 1½", 8", 15" and 21½". The rung grooves go across the underside at 3–6" and 16½–19½". If any T-nut lands inside a groove band, stop before cutting.

Check Widths, and metal in the way

The driver strip is 2½" and the middle about 2½". The passenger strip is 1½": if yours is wider, rip it, keeping its outside edge. Look for screws or old tongue bolts in the cut lines and the groove bands.

Label everything

D, M, P on the strips, TOP on their top faces, an arrow toward the hatch end. The matching letter on each riser.

Mark the back and front lines off the risers

Driver and middle strips: square a line across ½" forward of the riser's hatch-end edge, then another 22½" forward of that. Check it's 1" short of the riser's front end. Carry the back line across to the passenger strip with a straightedge and mark 22½" on it too.

Run the four checks (Section A)

Front lines behind the crossbar, T-nuts clear of 3–6" and 16½–19½", widths, and no metal in the lines or the bands.

Unbolt the risers

Bag each riser's bolts and washers with its letter. The T-nuts stay in the wood.

Cut

Cut the crossbar off first, then each strip on its back and front lines: three 22½" strips. Rip the passenger strip to 1½" if it's wider, keeping its outside edge. Chamfer the top edges at both ends. Then go to the Anchor Board sheet's grooves step.
