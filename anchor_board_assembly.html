Check before you cut

3 checks · 3 methods

Check Why there's no crossbar

From 26" forward the side trim closes the van to 42", and the fridge tray fills the space between the driver and middle strips for its whole length. A plywood crossbar could only reach the driver strip through a ½" neck. Steel rungs set into the strips' undersides pass under the tray (it hangs ⅜" over the seat; the rung is 3/16") and under the kitchen unit, so the strips stay one-piece and the board stays one unit.

Check The hoops are the seat latches

The four white hoops in the headrest trough, 28" from the hatch, with their ⅝" bars 1⅞" below the seat surface when it's loaded (2⅜" unloaded), are the 3rd-row seats' latch strikers: built to hold the seats in a crash. Measured from the driver line their centers are at 4¾", 24¾", 31¼" and 41¾" (H1–H4), each 3" wide.

H1 sits under the fridge tray's front corner: wrap its chain on before the tray assembly goes in. The hoops' bars measure ⅝", too thick for a ¼" shackle, which only closes around about ⅜".

Check The strips end past the seat

The seat surface is solid to 24¾"; the strips run to 25½", overhanging the trough by ¾" and leaving the riser's last ½" clear for the eye bolt. Every T-nut, the last at 24½", is over solid seat.

Method Chains from the risers' front ends

From each riser's front end down to its hoop is only about 5": too short for any ratchet strap, whose mechanism alone is longer. So each riser gets a forged ¼-20 shoulder eye bolt hanging down through a 5/16" hole ½" from its front end, just ahead of the strip. A ¼" quick link joins the eye to a short length of ¼" chain, and the chain wraps once around the hoop's ⅝" bar and a second ¼" quick link closes it back on itself: H1 for the driver riser, H2 for the kitchen riser. Turning the eye bolt's nut on top of the flange draws the chain tight; a second nut locks it. Tension it with the fridge loaded on its tray. The seat squashes about ½" under weight (the hoop bar is 2⅜" below the bare seat top, 1⅞" loaded), so a chain tightened on an empty board goes slack once the fridge is in. Turn the eye to face its hoop. The load goes hoop → chain loop → quick link → chain → eye bolt → riser → four bolts → strip → rungs.

Method Backup straps and the relief notches

The optional backup straps run from the board forward to the 3rd-row striker loops on the 2nd-row floor, under Panel C's front edge and Panel B's end rail. If you run them, cut 3"-wide relief notches there at 12¾–15¾" and 30¼–33¼" from the driver line: 1" up in Panel C's front wall, ½" in its front bottom rail and outer back blocks, ½" in Panel B's end bottom rail.

Method Strips from the old comb

Cut the crossbar off the old comb; each of its three strips is already one piece. Trim each to 22½". New ¾" plywood only if you'd rather start fresh.

Build order

Cut the strips

Cut the crossbar off the old comb. Trim the three strips to 22½". Chamfer the top edges at both ends.

Groove the undersides

Lay the strips upside down side by side, back ends even. Mark two 3-1/16"-wide bands across all three: 3"–6" and 16½"–19½" from their back ends (6"–9" and 19½"–22½" from the hatch). Router or table saw each band 3/16" deep. Check a rung sits flush.

Cut and drill the rungs

Cut both bars to 46". Lay the strips at their spacing (Fig 1: 0–2½", 21⅞–24⅜", 44½–46" from the driver line) and the rungs in their grooves. Mark four screw holes per crossing on the driver and middle strips, in a square: ⅝" in from each edge of the strip, and ¾" and 2¼" across the rung. Mark two on the passenger strip, centered on it, at ¾" and 2¼" across the rung. That's 10 per rung. Drill 3/16" through the steel (step bit), chamfer each hole with a ½" twist bit at low speed until the head sits flush, and deburr the back of each hole. Number each rung and its crossings so they go back where they were marked.

Rust-proof the rungs, then screw them on

The bars are hot-rolled steel. Their gray mill scale is no rust protection, and the drilling and chamfering leave bare steel. Under the board they sit on the seat's fabric, which holds damp, and rust there stains the seat. So finish them before they go on.

Wire-brush or scuff each rung all over (a flap disc or 80-grit), paying most attention to the cut ends and the holes. Wipe it with mineral spirits or denatured alcohol and let it dry. Spray one coat of rusty-metal primer, then one or two light coats of enamel. Do both faces and the edges, and don't load paint into the chamfers: the screw heads have to seat flush. Let it dry to the touch, about an hour, before handling, and give it a day before the screws go in so the paint doesn't tear around the heads. If the riser angles are still bare steel, do them in the same session.

Then set each rung back in its grooves, drill 3/32" pilots about 7/16" deep into the ply (tape flag; there's only 9/16" of wood above the groove), and screw up into the strips with #8 × ⅝" flat heads. Touch up any paint the screws chip.

T-nuts for the risers

Set the tray assembly on the board, risers on their strips, back ends ½" behind the strips' back ends. Mark through the 8 flange holes, then drill, counterbore and seat the T-nuts (Figs 4–5).

Eye-bolt holes in the risers

Drill 5/16" (step bit) through each riser's flange ½" from its front end, centered across the flange's flat. It lands just ahead of the strip's end, so the eye bolt hangs free. Eye bolt up from below, washer and nut on top, eye turned toward its hoop.

Strap anchors and neoprene

The passenger strip's footman loop lengthwise at 9½"–12½" from the hatch. On the middle strip, a short ¼-20 eye bolt in the kitchen riser's second T-nut, 11" from the hatch, in place of that bolt's button head: the strip's ½" outer band is too narrow for a loop. Cut the neoprene to each strip, ⅛" inside the edges, with gaps under the rungs.

In the van

Install

Neoprene, then the board, back ends 3" from the hatch. Wrap a chain around H1's bar, close it with a quick link through the end link and a link of the standing chain, and lay it forward before the tray goes in. Bolt the tray assembly down. Wrap the second chain around H2 the same way. Quick-link each chain to its eye bolt on the link that's just taut, with the fridge loaded on its tray, turn the nut down until the chain is tight and the board won't move forward, then jam a second nut on top. Re-check after the first drive. Kitchen unit in; front strap in its front notches to H2 and H4, rear strap in its rear notches to the riser's eye bolt and the passenger footman loop.
