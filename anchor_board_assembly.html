A

Check before you cut

3 checks · 3 methods

Check Why there's no crossbar

From 26" forward the side trim closes the van to 42", and the fridge tray fills the space between the driver and middle strips for its whole length. A plywood crossbar could only reach the driver strip through a ½" neck. Steel rungs set into the strips' undersides pass under the tray (it hangs ⅜" over the seat; the rung is 3/16") and under the kitchen unit, so the strips stay one-piece and the board stays one unit.

Check The hoops are the seat latches

The four white hoops in the headrest trough, 28" from the hatch and 2¾" below the seat surface, are the 3rd-row seats' latch strikers: built to hold the seats in a crash. Measured from the driver line their centers are at 4¾", 24¾", 31¼" and 41¾" (H1–H4), each 3" wide.

H1 sits under the fridge tray's front corner: hook its strap before the tray assembly goes in.

Check The strips end past the seat

The seat surface is solid to 24¾"; the strips run to 26", just short of the trim, overhanging the trough by 1¼". Every T-nut, the last at 24½", is over solid seat.

Method Straps from the risers' front ends

The risers cover the strips' tops, so the board's forward straps start at the risers: a hole in each riser's flange near its front end, sized to the shackle's pin, a ¼" anchor shackle through it, and a strap forward and down to H1 (driver) and H2 (kitchen side). The load goes hoop → strap → shackle → riser → four bolts → strip → rungs.

Method Backup straps and the relief notches

The optional backup straps run from the board forward to the 3rd-row striker loops on the 2nd-row floor, under Panel C's front edge and Panel B's end rail. If you run them, cut 3"-wide relief notches there at 12¾–15¾" and 30¼–33¼" from the driver line: 1" up in Panel C's front wall, ½" in its front bottom rail and outer back blocks, ½" in Panel B's end bottom rail.

Method Strips from the old comb

Cut the crossbar off the old comb; each of its three strips is already one piece. Trim each to 23". New ¾" plywood only if you'd rather start fresh.

Build order

Cut the strips

Cut the crossbar off the old comb. Trim the three strips to 23". Chamfer the top edges at both ends.

Groove the undersides

Lay the strips upside down side by side, back ends even. Mark two 2-1/16"-wide bands across all three: 3½"–5½" and 17"–19" from their back ends (6½"–8½" and 20"–22" from the hatch). Router or table saw each band 3/16" deep. Check a rung sits flush.

Cut and drill the rungs

Cut both bars to 46". Lay the strips at their spacing (Fig 1: 0–2½", 21⅞–24⅜", 44½–46" from the driver line) and the rungs in their grooves. Mark two screw holes per crossing on the 2½" strips, ½" either side of the rung's centerline, and one on the passenger strip. Drill 3/16" through the steel, countersink, and screw up into the strips with #10 × ⅝" flat heads.

T-nuts for the risers

Set the tray assembly on the board, risers on their strips, back ends ½" behind the strips' back ends. Mark through the 8 flange holes, then drill, counterbore and seat the T-nuts (Figs 4–5).

Shackle holes in the risers

Drill a hole in each riser's flange about ½" from its front end, centered across the flange's flat, just bigger than the shackle's pin: a ¼" shackle usually has a 5/16" pin, so 11/32" (step bit #1). Fit a ¼" anchor shackle through each.

Footman loops and neoprene

Footman loops lengthwise at 9½"–12½" from the hatch: on the middle strip's outer ¾" band and on the passenger strip. Cut the neoprene to each strip, ⅛" inside the edges, with gaps under the rungs.

In the van

Install

Neoprene, then the board, back ends 3" from the hatch. Hook a strap into H1 and lay it forward before the tray goes in. Bolt the tray assembly down. Strap the shackles to H1 and H2 and tension them. Kitchen unit in; front strap in its front notches to H2 and H4, rear strap in its rear notches to the two loops.
