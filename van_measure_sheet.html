Van measurements: foot test and hoop drop

Two things to measure in the van, 3rd row folded. They need the van in different states, so the order matters: do the hoop drop (2) first, with the anchor board out, then the foot test (1) with everything in.

1The feet that wobble

Do this with Panel C sitting in the van where it lives: back edge at the hatch. Do it with the anchor board in and its chains on: its strips lie on the flat seat surface and may stop the rocking on their own. If nothing rocks, no pads are needed.

A folded 3rd-row seat has a flat top with rounded edges, and there are gaps between the seats. By "flat seat surface" I mean the flat top, away from those rounded edges and gaps. A foot standing on a rounded edge rocks. A foot standing on the flat top doesn't.

  1. Push down on each corner of Panel C. Note which feet rock: FL and FR are the front (cab-end) corners, RL and RR the rear (hatch-end) corners; left is the driver side.
  2. For each one that rocks, slide a scrap of plywood under the foot. Keep trying longer pieces until the corner stops rocking when you push on it.
  3. Note the scrap size that worked, and which way it had to reach from the foot: toward the front, the back, the side, or the middle.
  4. If a foot sits over a gap between two seats, measure how wide the gap is.

The scrap only proves the size. Its thickness raises Panel C, so the real pad will probably be thinner: ⅛" steel or ¼" hardboard, cut to the size that worked.

2Hoop drop: what the chains have to reach

Each fridge riser gets an eye bolt hanging down from its front end, 26" from the hatch, and a short chain from it to a seat-latch hoop: H1 for the driver riser, H2 for the kitchen riser. The chain hardware is bought to fit, so measure both hoops first. Do it with the anchor board out and the seat top bare, from the hatch end.

Side view, looking at the driver side, hatch on the left. The board is out. The bar is the hoop's crossbar seen end-on; its two legs drop into the trough behind it.

  1. D, the drop. Done: 1⅞" with weight on the seat, 2⅜" without, the same at all four hoops (24 Oct). The loaded figure is the one that counts: the chains are tensioned with the fridge in. Lay a straightedge or level flat on the bare seat top so it reaches out over the trough above the hoop. Measure straight down from its underside to the top of the hoop's bar.
  2. T, the bar's thickness. Done: ⅝" (24 Oct). That's too thick for a ¼" shackle, so the chain wraps the bar and a second quick link closes it.
  3. G, the room under the bar. Done: ¾" (24 Oct), enough for the ¼" chain to wrap under. From the bar's underside down to whatever is below it, inside the hoop. The chain has to wrap under the bar, so it needs at least ½".
  4. E, the room under the eye bolt. Done: 2" at the hoop, and much deeper toward the hatch (24 Oct), so the eye at 26" hangs free. No eye bolt needed for this one: it measures the van, not the bolt. At 26" from the hatch, on the riser's line, measure from the straightedge down to whatever is below. The eye bolt's eye hangs about 1½" below the seat top, so it needs about 1¾". The riser lines are about ¾" in from the driver line (H1's chain) and about 23¼" (H2's).
  5. F, where the bar is. Done: 28" (24 Oct), as the survey said. Tape from the closed hatch along the seat to the center of the bar.
  6. Look along the path from the eye-bolt spot to the hoop: is anything in the way? A latch cover, a seat-belt buckle, a trim lip?

With D, F and the eye bolt's spot, the chain's length comes out by geometry, so the chain gets cut once. H1 is 4¾" from the driver line, under where the fridge tray's front corner will be. H2 is 24¾", just passenger-side of the middle strip.
