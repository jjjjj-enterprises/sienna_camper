A

Check before you cut

2 methods · 1 carry-over

Panel B is Panel A's frame with bottom rails on all four faces and no divider, so most of it is a repeat of what you already built. Three things are new: the bearer goes in drop-in, the bottom side rails sit higher than Panel A's, and the rail top gets set against Panel A.

Method The centre bearer drops in, so the spare tire can get past it

The centre bearer (two 2×2s side by side, at the centreline) splits the top opening, the only way into Panel B, into two holes:

(46 − 2 × 1-7/16 − 2⅞ bearer) ÷ 2 = 20⅛" wide × 26⅛" fore-aft · each

The spare is a 28½" disc, 6.4" thick. To pass through a hole it has to go through edge-on, and at 6.4" thick the largest disc that fits a 20⅛ × 26⅛" hole at any angle is 27.0". The spare is 1½" too big. With the bearer fixed in place, the spare couldn't be loaded or pulled out in an emergency, and a screwed bearer can't be undone in the van: its screws would go in through the end rails' outer faces, tight against Panels A and C.

So the bearer is drop-in. It rests on four 1" steel corner braces (L-brackets) screwed to the end rails' inner faces, as in Fig 4. It carries the halves exactly as before, since the load is straight down. Take the halves off, lift the bearer out, and the full 43⅛ × 26⅛" opening is back. That passes discs up to 45". No tools needed.

Why 1" braces and not 1½": the bearer sits on the brace's flat leg, so a brace set flush with the rail's underside leaves the bearer proud by the steel's thickness (about 1/16"). The bed halves would rock on it. And a 1½" brace's upright leg is taller than the 1-7/16" rail, so its tip can reach the rail top. Fit the braces with the frame upside down (step 09): the bearer's top and the rail tops both lie on the floor, so they come out flush whatever the steel is.

While you're checking the spare: lying flat it's 28½" against 26⅛" between the end rails. It fits only because it sits on its 3" cleats, above the bottom rails, and its edge can pass under the top end rails. That leaves ¼" to the frame's outer face at each end, so centre the skid fore-aft.

Method The bottom side rails sit 1¾" up the legs, so their screws miss everything

Unlike Panel A, Panel B has side bottom rails as well as end ones. The side rails butt into the legs' inner faces between them, and their only fixing is through the leg from its outer face, fore-aft, the same direction as the end rails' screws.

At the leg bottom, four screws would share the lower 1-7/16" of each leg with the ½" leveling-insert bore. So the side pair sits with its underside 1¾" above the leg bottom, just above the top of the bore. Its screws land about 2½" up, in solid wood, with nothing else nearby. The end rails stay at the leg bottom, exactly as on Panel A.

end-rail pair: ~¾" up, ⅜" either side of centre (as Panel A) · side-rail pair: ~2½" up, ⅜" either side of centre · any order

Why not raise the 46" end rails instead: the spare is 28½" across and there's only 26⅛" between the end rails, so its rim overhangs the bottom end rails at both ends. It clears them only because it rides on 3" cleats over their 2-7/16" tops. Any higher and the spare can't lie flat. The side rails are 4" outboard of the spare's edge, so raising them costs nothing.

Set the side rails with a 1¾" spacer block under each end, and pilot every hole.

Carry-over Set the rail top to Panel A, not to the tape measure

Panel A reads 16" from the floor to the rail bottom as built, so its rail top is about 17½". The deck plane needs 17¾" so the bed lands at 18½", flush with Panel C. Both panels share one mattress, so Panel B's rail top has to match Panel A's, whatever that turns out to be. Set Panel B's feet with the two frames side by side on the garage floor and a straightedge across the seam. Then raise both together by the same turn.

The bay height doesn't change with the feet: bay = L − 2 × 1-7/16 = 13⅞" for the 16¾" legs. The spare stack is 15.3" from the floor, which clears the top rail's underside by about 1" with the feet at 1".

Build order

same bench method as Panel A

Cut the end rails, then measure for the side rails

Two at 46". Stand them 29" apart outside to outside and cut the side rails to the gap, which should be about 26⅛". SPF varies board to board, so measure this panel's own gap.

Glue up the ring flat, in four corner clamps

Build it on the garage floor, like Panel A. Check the diagonals while the glue is still wet: both should read 54⅜". Equal diagonals are what let the A/B and B/C seams close flat later.

Two screws a corner, then the brackets

Pilot and countersink through the clamp, then drive 2 × 2½" SPAX through the end rail's face into the side rail's end, pulled below flush. The flat bracket goes inside the corner afterwards.

Lap the four legs

Put the 2" block against the side rail's inner face and the leg against the block. Lap the leg against the end rail's inner face with its top flush with the rail top. Clamp it, pilot, and drive 2 × 2½" through the rail face into the leg's side. The feet are already in, so skip that step.

Flip it, then fit the bottom side rails, 1¾" up

Measure between the legs (about 23¼") and cut. Cut a 1¾" spacer block. With the frame upside down, stand the spacer on each leg's bottom end and rest the rail on it, on the legs' line. That puts its underside 1¾" up from the leg bottom. Glue it, then drive 2 screws through each leg's outer face into the rail's end, about 2½" up and ⅜" either side of centre (Fig 3).

The 46" bottom end rails

Across the legs' outer faces, directly under the top end rails, with the underside flush with the leg bottoms, glued, exactly as on Panel A. Drive 2 screws per leg, about ¾" up and ⅜" either side of centre, to miss the insert bore. They sit well below the side-rail screws, so this step and the last can go in either order.

Diagonal braces on the underside

Two 4" straps at two opposite corners, on the underside of the top ring. Never on the rail tops, because the bed halves land there.

Footman loops, while it's upside down

Two 1" loops on the inside faces of the bottom rails, one each side of where the spare stack sits, for its cam strap. Optional now, but it's awkward to reach later.

The centre bearer, drop-in

Glue the two 2×2s face to face and let them cure clamped. Measure the gap between the end rails at the centreline, and cut the beam to that less ¼", about 25⅞".

With the frame still upside down, sit the bearer in the gap at the centreline, top face down on the floor, so it's flush with the rail tops. Set a 1" brace in each corner where the bearer meets an end rail: upright leg on the end rail's inner face, flat leg on the bearer's underside. Pilot and screw the braces to the rail only, never to the bearer. Lift the bearer out and back in once to check it drops freely.

Set the feet against Panel A

Stand it right way up beside Panel A and put a straightedge across the seam. Turn Panel B's feet until its rail tops match Panel A's, then take both to 17¾" together. The alignment pins and seam latches come later (Phase 7), once the three panels are mated.
