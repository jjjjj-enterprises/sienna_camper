corrections to Appendix J

Fix Measure the holes from the panel's ends

Appendix J gives 9" from each edge of the opening. Once the panel is centered, each end sits about 11/32" inside its channel, so on the panel itself the centers are 9 11/32" from each end (just under 9⅜"). Marking 9" from the panel's ends would push the intake ⅜" closer to the curved corner trim.

Watch The opening may narrow toward the glass

The opening measured 34" at the top but about 33⅜" at the glass line (a photo estimate). If it narrows, the panel's bottom corners will catch when you swing it in. Trim a corner only as much as it needs, and write down how much.

Steps

about an hour

Get the sheet flat

Glue or tape the overlap flat, especially along the board edge 15½" from the corner you'll use, where the intake hole crosses it. Check the long top edge runs straight across both boards; if they don't line up, snap a straight line and use that as the top.

Mark the rectangle

From the factory corner, measure 34 11/16" along the top edge and 9¾" down at both ends, then join the bottom corners. Check the diagonals: both should be about 36", equal within 1/16". Write FRONT on the right end, REAR on the left and INSIDE on the face.

Mark the holes

Draw a line across 4⅞" down from the top. On it, mark the exhaust center 9 11/32" from the REAR end and the intake center 9 11/32" from the FRONT end; check they're 16" apart. Draw the circles with a compass, or a string and pencil: exhaust radius 2 17/32", intake radius 3 1/16".

Cut it out

Box cutter and straightedge for the rectangle. Cut each hole about ⅛" inside its line, so you can open it up to fit the collar rather than cut it too big. Keep the offcuts for patching.

Fit it in the window

Glass all the way down. Top edge into the top channel, rear end all the way into its side channel, swing the front end in (Fig 3) and slide it toward the front until both ends are in about the same. Watch the bottom corners: if they catch, trim just that corner and note how much. Leave the glass down for now.

Close the glass on it

The glass meets the panel about 9" below the top of the opening. Past that there's only about ⅛" of give in the rubber, then the motor pushes against a solid panel. Don't use one-touch auto-up. Hold the switch until the glass is about ½" below the bottom edge; a window moves a few inches a second, so even a quick tap can overshoot that. Then use very short taps and stop as soon as the panel is held and won't wiggle: held, not squeezed. If the panel starts to bow, lower the glass a tap. While the template is in, watch whether the glass stops, reverses or keeps pushing when it meets the board, and note it below. That tells you how the real panel will be treated.

Taking it out

Lower the glass all the way before you touch the panel. Never open the sliding door with the panel or hoses in: the door slides back along the van and will tear the panel out or wrench the hoses.

Check the corner trim

From inside, look at the intake hole against the curved trim at the top front. There should be about ⅝" of board between them, enough for the collar's face to come through without touching the trim.

Try the collars

Open each hole until its collar pushes through from inside, snug but not forced. Notice how little the lip overlaps the hole; the real panel gets a clamp on both faces of each collar so the hose can't pull through.

Run the hoses and shut the door

Hoses to the WAVE 3: no kinks, and both pass the platform edge (7" of room there). Close the sliding door gently and check it latches without pressing on a hose.

Write it down

Fill in the table below and keep the template; it's the pattern for the plastic. Send the numbers back so Appendix J can be corrected.
